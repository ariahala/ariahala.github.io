Where to start

For a first overview, begin with Pigati’s summer school notes. Then choose a direction: Maggi for sets of finite perimeter, the currents references for oriented surfaces, or Simon and De Lellis for varifolds and regularity.

The anisotropic and tangent-cone sections are intended as a next step after this foundation. The comments on the introductory references reflect my own experience and taste.

Useful background: measure theory, multivariable analysis and basic functional analysis; differential forms for currents, and elliptic PDE for regularity.

02Sets of finite perimeter

Sets of finite perimeter, also called Caccioppoli sets, provide a way to treat hypersurfaces as boundaries of measurable sets. This is a natural starting point for isoperimetric problems and codimension-one regularity.

  • Sets of Finite Perimeter and Geometric Variational Problems

    Francesco Maggi · Graduate textbook

    This is my favorite book to start learning from: it develops the theory with many examples and detailed proofs. Begin with the measure-theoretic foundations, then move to finite perimeter, compactness and lower semicontinuity, and the regularity of perimeter minimizers. Measure theory and some functional analysis are useful background.

    Publisher

03Currents & regularity

Currents encode orientation and multiplicity and give a flexible formulation of Plateau’s problem. After the definitions, the key milestones are compactness, existence of minimizers, and ε-regularity.

  • Cartesian Currents in the Calculus of Variations I

    Mariano Giaquinta, Giuseppe Modica & Jiří Souček · Graduate textbook

    Although the book is devoted to Cartesian currents, it also gives an excellent introduction to currents and a useful crash course in measure theory and real analysis. The foundational material can be read before the specialized applications.

    Publisher

  • Geometric Integration Theory

    Steven G. Krantz & Harold R. Parks · Graduate textbook

    An accessible development from measure theory to currents, with detailed regularity arguments. This is a useful reference when working through the ε-regularity theory for area-minimizing integral currents.

    Publisher

  • Center Manifold: A Case Study

    Camillo De Lellis & Emanuele Spadaro · Focused reading: Appendix A

    Appendix A is a useful place to study De Giorgi’s ε-regularity theorem. Read it alongside a first course on area-minimizing currents, before moving to the more involved center-manifold constructions in higher codimension.

    arXiv

04Varifolds & Allard regularity

Varifolds retain tangent-plane information without requiring an orientation. First variation, monotonicity, rectifiability and Allard’s theorem form the core of this part of the subject.

  • Introduction to Geometric Measure Theory

    Leon Simon · Graduate text

    A concise, systematic reference for readers with some graduate analysis. It develops the theory of varifolds and gives a detailed treatment of Allard regularity in modern notation; it also provides the background for the tangent-cone references below.

    PDF

  • Allard’s Interior Regularity Theorem: An Invitation to Stationary Varifolds

    Camillo De Lellis · Expository notes

    A gentle introduction to integral varifolds and Allard’s regularity theorem, with a particularly useful presentation of the tilt-excess decay argument. A good companion to Simon’s text.

    Author’s page

  • On the First Variation of a Varifold

    William K. Allard · Foundational paper

    The foundational treatment of first variation, rectifiability, compactness and regularity for varifolds. It is helpful to read an exposition first, then return to the original paper for the full framework and hypotheses.

    Journal

05Anisotropic geometric measure theory

An anisotropic surface energy assigns a cost that can depend on position and tangent plane. The Wulff shape is the basic isoperimetric model; rectifiability, regularity and min–max theory require understanding what replaces the isotropic tools.

  • Sets of Finite Perimeter and Geometric Variational Problems

    Francesco Maggi · Starting point: Chapter 20

    Return to Chapter 20, “Anisotropic surface energies,” after learning finite-perimeter compactness. Section 20.2 treats the Wulff problem and gives a concrete first encounter with direction-dependent surface energies.

    Publisher

  • Rectifiability of Varifolds with Locally Bounded First Variation with Respect to Anisotropic Surface Energies

    Guido De Philippis, Antonio De Rosa & Francesco Ghiraldin · Research paper

    An extension of Allard’s rectifiability theory to anisotropic energies. The central structural hypothesis is the atomic condition; in codimension one, the paper relates it to strict convexity of the integrand.

    arXiv

  • The Anisotropic Min-Max Theory: Existence of Anisotropic Minimal and CMC Surfaces

    Guido De Philippis & Antonio De Rosa · Research paper

    A route from anisotropic first variation to a min–max construction in closed three-manifolds. The paper constructs surfaces of prescribed constant anisotropic mean curvature, smooth away from at most one point.

    arXiv

  • Existence and Regularity of Min-Max Anisotropic Minimal Hypersurfaces

    Guido De Philippis, Antonio De Rosa & Yangyang Li · Further developments

    Develops the min–max theory in higher dimensions, with control of the singular set. A key ingredient is a uniform upper bound for density ratios; in dimension three the construction gives a smooth anisotropic minimal surface.

    arXiv

Connections with my work

With Antonio De Rosa and Ling Wang, Min-Max Construction of Anisotropic Minimal Surfaces with Genus Bound develops an anisotropic Meeks–Simon–Yau theorem and a Simon–Smith construction with control of genus.

With Antonio De Rosa, The Anisotropic Weyl Law and Grassmannian Equidistribution studies the anisotropic volume spectrum and equidistribution of both position and tangent plane. The accompanying interactive cube-to-slab model illustrates one of the constructions.

06Tangent cones & singularities

Blowing up near a point reveals the local geometry of a surface. For area-minimizing currents, monotonicity and compactness lead to tangent cones; proving that the cone is unique is a separate, much subtler question.

  • Introduction to Geometric Measure Theory

    Leon Simon · Starting point: tangent cones & densities

    Start with monotonicity and blow-ups, then Chapter 7, §3, “Tangent Cones and Densities.” Keep existence of a subsequential limit separate from uniqueness of the limit.

    PDF

  • Asymptotics for a Class of Non-Linear Evolution Equations, with Applications to Geometric Problems

    Leon Simon · Advanced: the Łojasiewicz–Simon method

    A foundational source for the analytic approach to asymptotic uniqueness. Its geometric applications include uniqueness of tangent cones in settings with a smooth multiplicity-one link; this is a natural next step after the basic blow-up theory.

    Journal

  • Uniqueness of Tangent Cones for 2-Dimensional Almost Minimizing Currents

    Camillo De Lellis, Emanuele Spadaro & Luca Spolaor · Advanced: two-dimensional currents

    A treatment of uniqueness for two-dimensional almost area-minimizing currents, extending the ideas of White’s theorem for area-minimizing currents. Useful for seeing how quantitative comparison arguments produce uniqueness.

    arXiv

  • Uniqueness of Certain Cylindrical Tangent Cones

    Gábor Székelyhidi · Advanced: cylindrical singularities

    Studies uniqueness when an area-minimizing hypersurface has tangent cone C × ℝ, with C the Simons cone over S³ × S³. The singular axis and the failure of integrability make this a different problem from cones with an isolated singularity.

    arXiv

A related construction

My paper with Luca Spolaor, On Cylindrical Singularities of Minimal Hypersurfaces, constructs a minimal hypersurface in a smooth conformally Euclidean metric with isolated singularities accumulating at a cylindrical singularity. It provides a concrete example to keep in mind when studying singular sets and tangent cones.
