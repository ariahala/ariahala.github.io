03 / LOOK INSIDE THE MAP

The four cut-and-translate steps

Intersection width

The unit square first scales uniformly by ρ=ν. Each subsequent operation only translates pieces. Panels show the fillings, including their outer edges; dashed lines show the final piece partition at every stage. At ν=1, the translations cancel in the final panel, giving the identity map.

Drawing, fillings, and the construction ↗

Drawing a relative cycle

Open strokes are extended to the nearest edges of the square. The two ends are joined along the shorter boundary arc to select a filling. This boundary arc is not part of the relative cycle. Close a stroke near its starting point to make a loop.

Multiple strokes combine by symmetric difference (coefficients in ℤ2). Use Flip filling to choose the complementary filling. Presets provide a keyboard-accessible alternative to freehand drawing.

The map used here

For each slab–cube intersection, ν is its width. We apply the paper’s planar map Tν to the filling and translate the result into that intersection. The final curve is the relative boundary of all assembled fillings, with matching edges cancelled modulo 2.

The outer boundary of Q is omitted. Simultaneously complementing every input changes the shading but leaves Ψ unchanged. At ν=0 the image is empty, and at ν=1 the map is the identity.
