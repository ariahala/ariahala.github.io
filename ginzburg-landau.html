Geometric Measure Theory

Where to start

Begin with Bethuel–Brezis–Hélein for the planar vortex picture, then study Jacobians and energy bounds before moving to higher dimensions.

For the direction closest to my work, follow self-dual abelian Higgs vortices into Pigati–Stern and Parise–Pigati–Stern, then the stability and excess-decay papers. The magnetic GL monograph is a complementary route.

Useful background: Sobolev spaces, elliptic PDE, variational methods and degree theory; differential forms and connections for abelian Higgs, and currents and varifolds for geometric limits.

01Non-magnetic GL: planar vortices

In the planar non-magnetic model, the order parameter is a complex-valued map. Its modulus is driven toward one, while topological constraints force zeros around which the phase winds.

  • Ginzburg–Landau Vortices

    Fabrice Bethuel, Haïm Brezis & Frédéric Hélein · Starting point · Graduate monograph

    The classical starting point for two-dimensional vortices. The book develops the asymptotics of minimizers, the emergence of point defects and the renormalized energy governing their interaction. It is a great place to begin learning the theory.

    Publisher

02Jacobians & variational convergence

The Jacobian records the vorticity in a form that survives weak convergence. It connects energy estimates for maps with measures and integral currents, and makes the link with GMT precise.

  • The Jacobian and the Ginzburg–Landau Energy

    Robert L. Jerrard & Halil Mete Soner · Foundational paper

    A central reference for compactness of Jacobians and lower bounds for the rescaled Ginzburg–Landau energy. Read this after the planar vortex picture to understand how vorticity can be controlled without locating each zero individually.

    PDF

  • Variational Convergence for Functionals of Ginzburg–Landau Type

    Giovanni Alberti, Sisto Baldo & Giandomenico Orlandi · Research paper · GL and integral currents

    A variational convergence framework relating Ginzburg–Landau-type energies to area. The formulation in terms of integral currents explains how topological singularities of maps give rise to geometric objects of higher codimension.

    cvGMT PDF

03η-ellipticity & higher dimensions

η-ellipticity is a small-energy principle: sufficiently small normalized energy forces the modulus to stay away from zero in a smaller region. It is a key tool for locating the concentration set as the core scale tends to zero.

  • Asymptotics for the Ginzburg–Landau Equation in Arbitrary Dimensions

    Fabrice Bethuel, Haïm Brezis & Giandomenico Orlandi · Research paper · Elliptic GL

    A foundational reference for the higher-dimensional equation at logarithmic energy scale. It develops the estimates and concentration analysis behind the passage from vortices to codimension-two geometric limits.

    cvGMT

GMT background for this step

The language of currents describes vorticity and topology, while varifolds describe energy concentration and stationarity. Keeping these two roles in view helps when comparing the non-magnetic and abelian Higgs limits.

04Magnetic Ginzburg–Landau

Introducing a gauge field changes the energy balance and allows one to study applied magnetic fields and vortex distributions. This is a useful companion to the non-magnetic theory, with its own scaling regimes.

  • Vortices in the Magnetic Ginzburg–Landau Model

    Étienne Sandier & Sylvia Serfaty · Graduate monograph

    A systematic treatment of the magnetic model. The chapters on the vortex-ball construction and Jacobian estimates are especially useful for learning the energy methods; later chapters study applied-field regimes and vortex distributions.

    Publisher

05Abelian Higgs: self-dual vortices

Here the focus is the self-dual U(1) Yang–Mills–Higgs model. The unknowns are a section of a Hermitian line bundle and a connection. In two dimensions, the Bogomolny decomposition leads to first-order vortex equations and a topological energy bound.

  • Arbitrary N-Vortex Solutions to the First Order Ginzburg–Landau Equations

    Clifford Henry Taubes · Foundational paper · Planar self-dual vortices

    The basic existence and uniqueness result for prescribed vortex zeros in the plane, with multiplicities. This supplies the two-dimensional family of minimizers that underlies quantitative stability questions.

    Journal

From classification to stability

Once the minimizing vortices are understood, one can ask how close a pair with almost minimal energy must be to that family. This is the question in my quantitative stability paper below.

06From abelian Higgs to minimal submanifolds

For the self-dual rescaling, bounded energy leads to a codimension-two limit. This differs from the logarithmic normalization in non-magnetic GL: the gauge field and the coupling are essential to the comparison.

  • Minimal Submanifolds from the Abelian Higgs Model

    Alessandro Pigati & Daniel Stern · Research paper · Critical points and varifolds

    Shows that, as the core scale tends to zero, uniformly energy-bounded critical points have subsequential normalized energy measures given by stationary integral varifolds of codimension two. A natural bridge from gauge-theoretic PDE to the compactness and regularity questions of GMT.

    arXiv

  • Convergence of the Self-Dual U(1)-Yang–Mills–Higgs Energies to the (n−2)-Area Functional

    Davide Parise, Alessandro Pigati & Daniel Stern · Research paper · Γ-convergence

    Complements the critical-point picture with Γ-convergence to codimension-two area. The gauge-invariant Jacobian converges to an integral cycle, and the recovery construction realizes cycles in the prescribed homology class.

    arXiv

07Connections with my work

The references above lead to two related questions: quantitative control near planar vortices, and improvement of flatness for higher-dimensional abelian Higgs fields.

  • Quantitative Stability for Yang–Mills–Higgs Instantons in Two Dimensions

    Aria Halavati · My work · Quantitative stability

    After Taubes’s classification, the next question is quantitative: does a small energy deficit control the distance to a minimizing vortex? This paper establishes a quadratic stability estimate for nearly minimizing pairs, including a corresponding result on compact surfaces.

    PDF arXiv Slides

  • Decay of Excess for the Abelian Higgs Model

    Guido De Philippis, Aria Halavati & Alessandro Pigati · My work · Allard-type improvement of flatness

    Building on the codimension-two limit, we prove an Allard-type improvement of flatness and apply it to entire critical points with normalized energy close to that of a multiplicity-one plane. The results include uniqueness of blow-down and two-dimensionality under the dimension or minimizing assumptions in the paper.

    PDF arXiv Slides

  • New Weighted Inequalities on Two-Manifolds

    Aria Halavati · Related analytic tools

    Weighted Poincaré and elliptic inequalities on surfaces, motivated by estimates for a weighted Hodge decomposition. These provide analytic background for the stability questions above.

    PDF arXiv
